How to Create a Professional CV Online for Free

A clear, well-organized CV helps employers understand your skills, education and experience. This practical guide explains how to prepare a professional CV, what information to include and how to save it as a PDF without paying for expensive software.

CV Tools Editorial Guide · Updated November 2026 · 8–10 minute read
📄

Creating a CV can feel difficult, especially when you are applying for your first job or changing careers. You may have useful skills and experience but not know how to present them clearly. The good news is that you do not need complicated design software to prepare a professional-looking document.

The most important parts of a strong CV are relevant information, clear writing, readable formatting and accurate details. A decorative design cannot replace these basics.

1. Before You Start Writing Your CV

Start by understanding the job you want. Read the job description carefully and identify the responsibilities, qualifications and skills the employer mentions. This helps you decide which parts of your background are most relevant.

Prepare your information before choosing a template. Collect your education details, employment dates, achievements, projects, training and contact information. Check spelling, dates and job titles so you do not have to correct them later.

Useful tip: Keep a master document containing all your experience and achievements. You can then select the most relevant information for each job application.

2. Essential Sections of a Professional CV

A CV should make important information easy to find. Most job applications benefit from the following sections, although the exact structure depends on your experience and the role.

Contact Information

Include your full name, a professional email address, phone number and location such as your city and country. Add a LinkedIn profile or portfolio link when it is relevant and up to date.

You usually do not need to include sensitive personal details such as your national identity number, bank information or full home address.

Professional Summary

Write a short introduction describing your professional background, strongest relevant skills and career focus. It should help the reader quickly understand what you can offer.

Work Experience

List relevant roles in reverse chronological order, starting with your most recent position. Include your job title, employer, location and dates. Use short bullet points to explain your responsibilities and results.

Education

Include your qualification, institution and completion date or expected graduation date. Students and recent graduates may place education near the top of their CV.

Skills

Choose skills that are relevant to the role. You can include technical abilities, software knowledge, languages and job-related interpersonal skills when you can support them with examples.

Optional Sections

Depending on your background, you may also include certifications, projects, volunteer work, professional memberships, publications or relevant awards. Only add sections that provide useful information to the employer.

3. How to Write a Professional Summary

A professional summary is usually two to four concise sentences near the beginning of your CV. Avoid vague claims such as “I am hardworking and want a good job.” Explain your background and strengths instead.

Example for an entry-level applicant

Recent business graduate with academic experience in market research, spreadsheet analysis and customer service projects. Interested in an entry-level administrative role where strong organization, communication and problem-solving skills can support daily operations.

Example for an experienced applicant

Customer service professional with four years of experience supporting customers, resolving service issues and maintaining accurate records. Experienced in CRM systems, team coordination and handling customer enquiries across multiple communication channels.

These are examples to adapt, not statements to copy if they do not match your actual background. Be honest about your experience and abilities.

4. How to Describe Your Work Experience

Do not simply list every task you performed. Focus on relevant responsibilities, contributions and outcomes. Begin bullet points with clear action verbs such as organized, prepared, supported, managed, developed or coordinated.

Weak description

Responsible for social media and posts.

More informative description

Prepared weekly social media content, scheduled posts and monitored audience comments to support the company's online communication.

Where possible, include accurate numbers or measurable results. Never invent percentages, sales figures or achievements just to make a CV appear stronger.

If you have held several jobs, give more space to recent and relevant positions. Older or unrelated work can be summarized briefly.

5. How to Choose the Right Skills

Read the job advertisement and identify the skills that genuinely match your experience. Separate technical skills from broader professional abilities if that makes the information easier to scan.

  • Technical skills: Spreadsheet software, bookkeeping, graphic design, programming or data entry.
  • Communication skills: Written communication, presentations and customer support.
  • Organization skills: Scheduling, record keeping and task coordination.
  • Language skills: List languages honestly and describe your proficiency accurately.

Avoid adding a long list of unrelated skills. A focused list supported by your education, projects or work history is more useful than a page full of keywords.

6. CV Tips for Students and Fresh Graduates

You can create a credible CV even if you have never had a full-time job. Employers may also consider academic projects, internships, volunteering, part-time work and practical training.

If you are a student or recent graduate, consider including:

  • Your degree, institution and expected or completed graduation date.
  • Relevant coursework or academic projects that demonstrate useful abilities.
  • Internships, volunteer activities or part-time responsibilities.
  • Certifications and practical training related to the position.
  • Technical and communication skills you can demonstrate.

Do not label yourself as experienced if you are not. A clear entry-level CV that honestly presents your strengths is better than an exaggerated one.

7. CV Formatting: Keep It Clear and Readable

Use a simple layout with consistent headings, spacing and font sizes. Employers should be able to find your contact details, experience and education without searching through unnecessary design elements.

  • Use a readable font and consistent heading styles.
  • Keep text aligned and leave enough white space.
  • Use bullet points for responsibilities and achievements.
  • Avoid excessive colours, decorative graphics and complicated tables.
  • Use clear section names such as Work Experience, Education and Skills.
  • Choose a length that suits your experience and the employer's instructions.

There is no single CV length that suits every country, profession and application. Follow the employer's instructions where provided. For many early-career applications, a concise one-page CV may be sufficient; candidates with more relevant experience may need additional space.

8. Common CV Mistakes to Avoid

  • Spelling and grammar errors: Proofread your CV and ask someone else to review it when possible.
  • Unclear dates: Use a consistent date format for education and employment.
  • Generic content: Adapt your summary and relevant skills to the position.
  • Unnecessary personal details: Avoid including private information that the employer does not need.
  • Unverified claims: Do not invent qualifications, responsibilities or achievements.
  • Hard-to-read formatting: Avoid tiny fonts, crowded paragraphs and complex layouts.
  • Broken links: Test your email, portfolio and professional profile links before sending the CV.

9. How to Save and Check Your CV as a PDF

PDF is commonly used for job applications because it helps preserve the document's layout across different devices. However, always follow the file format requested by the employer.

  1. Finish editing your CV and check all personal details.
  2. Use the download or export option in your CV editor.
  3. Save the document as a PDF if PDF is accepted by the employer.
  4. Open the saved PDF and review every page.
  5. Check that headings, bullet points and contact details are visible.
  6. Confirm that no text is cut off and that links work where applicable.
  7. Use a clear filename, such as FirstName-LastName-CV.pdf.

Important: Some employers use applicant tracking systems (ATS) to organize applications. A simple, text-based layout with standard headings is generally easier for software to read, but no template can guarantee an ATS result.

10. Final CV Checklist

Before submitting your application, review this checklist:

  • My name and contact information are correct.
  • My summary matches the type of role I am applying for.
  • My experience and education dates are accurate.
  • My skills are relevant and truthful.
  • My achievements and responsibilities are clearly explained.
  • Spelling, grammar and formatting have been checked.
  • My CV is easy to read on a computer and phone.
  • The exported PDF opens correctly and has no missing content.
  • The filename is clear and professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I create a professional CV online for free?

Yes. You can use a free online CV builder or a document editor. Prepare your information, select a readable layout and check the exported file before applying.

What should I include in a CV if I have no work experience?

Focus on your education, relevant projects, internships, volunteering, training, practical skills and achievements. Include only information that accurately represents your background.

Should I send my CV as a PDF or Word document?

Follow the employer's instructions. If no format is specified, PDF is often a practical choice because it generally preserves layout. Some application systems may request a Word document instead.

How long should a professional CV be?

There is no universal page limit. Keep the CV focused and relevant. Your career stage, profession, country and the employer's requirements should guide its length.

Does a professional CV guarantee an interview?

No. A well-prepared CV can communicate your qualifications clearly, but interview decisions depend on the employer, role requirements, competition and other parts of the application.
